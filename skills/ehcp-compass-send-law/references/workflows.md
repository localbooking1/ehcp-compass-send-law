# Matter workflows

## Assessment request or refusal
Separate the test for conducting an assessment from the test for issuing a plan. Find and read each applicable statutory test. Map facts and professional evidence to it. Do not add a diagnosis, spending threshold, school application requirement or fixed number of SEN-support cycles unless the actual law establishes it. Draft a request addressing the relevant test and identify missing evidence.

## Plan review
Build a table: need; evidence/page; proposed provision; frequency/duration/provider expertise; outcome; plan section; unresolved gap. Review education/health/social-care classification on substance and verified law rather than the professional's job title. Check placement and transport issues separately. Avoid declaring every vague phrase unlawful without reading the evidence and applicable authority.

## Delay or missing provision
Create a dated chronology and actual-versus-specified provision record. Identify the responsible public body and current duty. Separate an adequacy dispute about plan contents from failure to deliver an existing plan. Research the appropriate complaint, appeal or urgent public-law route and the interaction of jurisdictions. Quantify missed sessions only from evidence.

## Review, amendment, cessation, transfer
Identify exactly which decision has occurred and whether the document is draft or final. Verify process, consultation and notices. Check the relevant clock and appeal rights rather than carrying over the assessment timeline. Do not assume a plan ends automatically on a birthday or a school move.

## Appeal preparation
Confirm jurisdiction and appealable decision, current forms and rules, mediation requirements/exceptions and deadline. Identify disputed sections, desired changes, evidence, witnesses, case-management directions and working-document format. Separate binding education determinations from health/social-care recommendations where relevant, after checking current law. Explain permission/error-of-law routes separately from a fresh merits appeal.

## Complaint drafting
Read the applicable procedure; choose the correct respondent and route. Distinguish corporate, statutory social-care, school, NHS, ICO and Ombudsman processes. Set out evidence, impact, requested remedy and a proportionate response request. Do not invent escalation stages or convert a local service standard into a statutory deadline. Check concurrent appeal/JR time limits before suggesting waiting.

## Discrimination, exclusion, attendance or transport
Identify school type, age, decision maker and forum first. Avoid assuming an EHCP is necessary for all equality or transport rights. Do not equate a school refusal with lawful exclusion. Check current guidance, statutes, remedies and deadlines independently for each issue.

## Data and case materials
Distinguish the child's personal-data rights, an authorised representative's request and separate parental education-record rights. Check current ICO guidance and amended data legislation. Use minimum necessary data, redact identifiers in examples, and do not send family details in public search queries. A SAR must not be assumed to pause an appeal deadline.

## Final audit
Review whether each legal claim is supported by a source actually read, each date has an evidenced trigger, each case is checked beyond its name, and each allegation is properly attributed. If the current-law gate fails, supply a factual draft with clear unresolved points rather than a purportedly final legal opinion.
